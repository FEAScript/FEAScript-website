GAME Story campaign · single player

Critical Mesh

Athens, 2089. The city runs on a single glowing plate of alloy, and you're its new night engineer. Roll the die, carry heat cells across the floor and mount them, then watch FEAScript solve the temperature under your boots. Push your luck too far, and the Plate melts down.

  • A story campaign of 10 night shifts and 3 engineering interludes, plus an endless mode with high scores
  • Four FEAScript models: heat conduction (with a Gmsh mesh), beam bending, front propagation and Stokes flow
  • The equation is always on screen, and 15 Field Notes explain the physics as you meet it

NO CODE For young engineers · in class or at home

FEM Blocks

Snap pixel blocks together to build a simulation: a shape, a material, heaters and what happens at every edge. Press Run and FEAScript solves it. Tri, a small triangle robot, guides you through nine missions, from a warm wire to a swirling tank of honey.

  • Click any block to see which part of the equation it controls
  • Every program is real FEAScript code: see it, copy it, use it
  • "The math" tab explains the equation, then shows your mesh, your nodes and your matrix K

What you learn

The ideas behind every simulation.

Engineers use the finite element method to design bridges, engines, phones and hearts. Its big ideas are simple enough to play with.

−∇·(k ∇T) = Q

An equation is a balance

Heat in, heat out, heat made inside. The heat equation just says that, at every point.

T = T₀ or ∂T/∂n = 0

Edges decide the answer

A fixed temperature, cooling air or a perfect blanket: the boundary conditions are half the problem.

T ≈ Σ Tᵢ Nᵢ

Cut it into pieces

A mesh of small elements turns an impossible problem into many easy ones, linked at the nodes.

K · T = F

Solve them all at once

One equation per node, stacked into one big table that the computer solves in well under a second.

k = 400 vs 0.03

Materials matter

Copper is a heat highway, foam a muddy path. That's why pans are metal and handles are wood.

beam · front · flow

One method, many physics

The same building blocks bend beams, grow solid fronts and stir fluids.

For teachers

Ready for the classroom.

Both apps run in any modern browser, with nothing to install and no sign-up. Progress is saved only in the student's own browser. FEM Blocks projects can be saved to a file and shared.
