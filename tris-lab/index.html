What you learn

The ideas behind every simulation.

Engineers use the finite element method (FEM) to design bridges, engines and phones. Both apps are built on six of its big ideas, and each one is simple enough to try for yourself.

−∇·(k ∇T) = Q

Every equation is a balance

Physics equations say that something balances at every point: heat in a plate, forces in a beam, fluid in a pipe. In the heat equation, the heat flowing in, flowing out and made there all add up.

T = T₀ or ∂T/∂n = 0

The edges matter

An edge can be held at a fixed temperature, cooled by air, or insulated so no heat escapes. These boundary conditions change the whole answer.

T ≈ Σ Tᵢ Nᵢ

Split the shape into elements

A shape too complicated to solve in one go is split into many small, simple pieces called elements. They meet at points called nodes, and the temperature is worked out at each node.

K · T = F

Solve all the nodes together

Each node gets one equation, linked to its neighbours. FEAScript solves all of them at once, usually in well under a second.

copper k ≈ 400 · foam k ≈ 0.03

Materials change the result

The conductivity k says how easily heat moves through a material. Copper spreads heat fast, foam almost stops it. That's why pans are metal and their handles aren't.

beam · front · flow

One method, many problems

Swap the equation and the same steps solve other problems: how a beam bends, how molten metal freezes and how a thick fluid flows.
